# Twenty Nanometres: critique log

(The v2 critique remains in ../../v2/twenty-nanometres/CRITIQUE.md.)

## Round 2 (v3), against Nimrod's notes

**What the round asked for:**
- the cleft as focal negative space between unequal, cropped fields;
- a visible crossing and consequence;
- a word that emerges from the network instead of sitting as a slogan;
- less antique filter;
- a still first.

**What now works:**
- The template is broken. On the 36-sheet, cleft angle, side, position and field sizes vary enough that no two neighbouring cells share a layout. The central horizontal fan of v2 is mostly gone.
- The partially lit word is the series' first real idea. Examples:
  - #05 HOME: only the M is lit.
  - #12 STAY: the lit lower letters against latent graphite.
  - #27 CALL ME: the word glows along the shore it was reached from.
  - #31 UNREAD: a failed spark stalls in the gap and the word stays silver and unread.
  At its best the word is half-present until the signal makes it legible.
- Removing the paper stains helped a great deal. Darkfield in particular reads as light and structure rather than as a filter.
- Depth now has a job: the cleft is sharp, everything inland falls away.

**What is still weak (honest):**
1. **Legibility is uneven.**
   - About a quarter of outputs read poorly at thumbnail size. Examples: #06 SORRY, #11 GOODBYE, #18 CALL ME (Failed), #35 DEADLINE.
   - The trunks that feed the letters enter from the soma side and merge into the letter strokes, especially when the receiving cell is "above" the word.
   - Long words at steep angles get small.
2. **The consequence is sometimes too local.** With Arrival (the most common Moment, 17/36) and a contact at the word's end, only a fragment lights, and the eye may not find it. Brightfield vermilion on thin branches is quieter than Darkfield warmth.
3. **The sending side is thin.** In Receiver Dominant outputs the pre arbour can read as a few stray lines rather than a field. The cleft then reads as "space next to a tree", not between two things.
4. **Spark scale is modest.** The DBM crossing is small next to the word. This is truer to the brief (the gap is the subject) but less dramatic. `Storm` and `Repeated` don't differ enough from `Crossed` at sheet scale.
5. **Train sparks are repetitive.** 3–5 near-parallel leaders read as a motif, not an event.
6. **Out-of-focus trunks are heavy.** In Darkfield the blurred inland trunks are large grey shapes that compete with the cleft in some outputs (#20, #32).
7. **Near Miss is rare** (2/36) and not yet obviously a miss.
8. **Verdict.** This proves the cleft composition in a still, as asked. It is not yet a finished long-form; see PLAN.

**Next round (proposed):**
- end letter guides at the glyph's shore-side edge, so trunks don't cross strokes;
- a minimum em and a word-length cap per angle;
- raise the sender attractor density in Receiver Dominant;
- stronger lit/unlit contrast in Brightfield;
- make Near Miss land visibly close to a letter;
- Storm: more contacts, and shorter, brighter leaders.
